Physics-Informed Conic Decomposition (PICD): A guarded solver layer for linear, nonlinear, and time-dependent problems

Physics-Informed Conic Decomposition (PICD) is a solver-layer construction placed around standard iterative methods rather than a replacement for them. The layer keeps a state over cone or block decompositions, residual history, and graph structure, then uses bounded gravity-like weights, transport-like congestion summaries, swarm-style adap- tation, residual correlation, and graph coarse information to alter preconditioner assembly, accepted-step damping, and coarse correction. The inner classical method is left intact dur- ing each solve. This paper states the mathematical parts of the design that are explicit in the current implementation, gives the algorithmic steps needed to apply PICD, and re- ports measurements from the repository that serves as the validation harness. The current repository run passes 98 out of 98 tests and completes 22 out of 22 examples. The current same-method publication benchmark suite favors PICD in both wall time and peak memory on every published pair. The full shipped linear example corpus contains 15 same-method pairs, with PICD faster on 14 of them in the latest run. The nonlinear and transient exam- ples are mixed: several direct pairs favor PICD, one is effectively even, and two are slower on this run. That sets the scope of the paper's performance claims.
Rohan R.
